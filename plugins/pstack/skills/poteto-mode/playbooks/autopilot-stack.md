### Autopilot-stack

**You own the stack, never the landing. Hand the operator one linear stack of base-chained PRs to review and land.** **Autopilot-full** holds except for how verified work leaves. Each tick re-reads both playbooks from disk under the installed plugin, and this one wins where they differ.

1. **Run Autopilot-full's owners, minus the landing.** Owners report STACK-READY in place of merge-ready. They never rebase onto another branch, trunk included, and never retarget, merge, close a PR, or arm auto-merge, because the root owns the chain's topology and the operator owns the landing. When an item depends on another, its owner branches from that item's branch, not from trunk. On the operator's go, write the full program objective into the standing orders and restate it in your todolist, and have each dynamic-mode `/loop` tick from Autopilot-full step 6 re-read this playbook and that objective. Record each verdict's head SHA, base SHA, base branch, and stable base-to-head patch-id, so a later rewrite can be compared with the reviewed context.
2. **Give the topology one writer.** Owners push only their own branches and report each tip and its intended parent. Only you rebase or retarget a link, because two writers on one chain undo each other. Before any rewrite or retarget, follow [Merge and restack safety](../references/merge-safety.md): capture the chain and its remote heads, cancel auto-merge requests and queue membership for that chain, and verify the cancellation. Append each clean PR in dependency order, with its base set to its parent's branch (`origin pr edit <pr> --base <parent-branch>` or `gh pr edit <pr> --base <parent-branch>`), and push each rewrite with `--force-with-lease=refs/heads/<branch>:<captured-remote-head>` against the remote SHA captured before rewriting. Never require `gt`. An owner takes your push as its new base and lease, and on a conflict resolves its own slice for you to push.
3. **Absorb drift at the root.** Until landing starts, apply Autopilot-full step 5's test to each link and rebase the chain bottom-up where it calls for one. Then apply the patch-id rule link by link, since a link's base is its parent. A verdict whose applicability to the new base is uncertain goes back through Autopilot-full step 4 before delivery.
4. **Deliver the chain, then keep your hands off it.** Each link carries its verdict in its PR. Once landing starts, nobody writes to the chain except the post-merge retarget that `playbooks/shipping.md` sanctions, because a second writer breaks a landing in flight. A mid-landing problem goes through you to the operator, and any fix waits for the operator's go.

**Choosing between the autopilots.** Autopilot-full when the PRs are independent and landing authority is granted. Autopilot-stack when the operator wants review before landing, the work is sequenced or coupled, or merge authority is withheld.

**Reply:** catch the operator up per the **ketchup** skill, with links to the stack root and tip, a one-line verdict per link, and anything parked or excluded with the reason.
